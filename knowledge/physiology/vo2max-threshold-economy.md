## Key takeaways

- Three different "thresholds" get sold as one thing. The planner must keep them apart: **V̇O₂max** (a ceiling of oxygen uptake), **maximal lactate steady state / threshold** (the highest *sustainable* intensity), and **critical speed** (a model parameter fitted from 2–3 exhaustive efforts).
- Threshold intensity in trained runners typically sits **below** vVO₂max — commonly reported around 90–95% of vVO₂max, and closer to vVO₂max only in the well-trained. **Never treat "100% vVO₂max pace" as threshold pace.**
- Running economy is the trainable part of "same oxygen, faster". It improves slowly (weeks–months) and survives breaks better than V̇O₂max does.
- Durability — the ability to hold pace/HR/economy late in a session — is measurable in the field and predicts performance beyond a single threshold number. It is the best available proxy for "ready for the back half of a race".
- Field-derived thresholds carry error of the same order as the training effect the plan is trying to create. Every threshold change must clear the noise floor (see `signals/decoupling-and-field-tests.md`).

## Definitions

- **V̇O₂max** — maximal rate of oxygen uptake; a whole-body ceiling, reached after ~2–4 min at high intensity in a progressive test. Expressed mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (absolute L·min⁻¹ when mass matters). In running the more useful derived quantity is **vVO₂max** (the speed at that ceiling).
- **MLSS (maximal lactate steady state)** — the highest exercise intensity at which blood lactate stays stable (commonly defined as a rise <1 mmol·L⁻¹) over the later part of a 30–120 min constant load. It is the physiologically honest definition of "threshold" and it is *a range of intensities*, reached by 2–3 separate tests.
- **LT₁ / LT₂ (lactate turnpoints)** — practical surrogates: LT₁ where lactate first rises above baseline (~+1.0 mmol·L⁻¹ over resting, or an absolute ~2.0–2.5 mmol·L⁻¹), LT₂/MLSS-identified where it climbs steeply (the fixed **4.0 mmol·L⁻¹ OBLA** point is a convention, not a physiological boundary).
- **Critical speed / power (CS/CP)** — the asymptote of the hyperbolic time–velocity relationship, estimated from 2–3 exhaustive efforts of ~2–15 min; the "W′" analogue is the finite work above it. Field CS from a 3-min all-out test or from 3- and 9-min efforts typically lands **near MLSS**, but the estimates disagree by a few percent between protocols.
- **Running economy** — oxygen (or energy) cost of running at a given submaximal speed. Better economy = faster at the same physiological load.
- **Durability** — the retained ability to produce force/pace at a given physiological cost late in exercise, measured as drift in HR, pace or economy between early and late matched blocks.

## Facts

- Threshold and V̇O₂max are **separable**: two runners with the same V̇O₂max can differ by minutes over 10 km because of threshold fraction and economy. Training blocks target different components, and progress in one does not guarantee progress in the other.
- The intensity ladder (relative, trained recreational runner, `confidence: medium` — coaching/textbook consensus [PHYS-S39, PHYS-S38]):
  | Session intent | Anchor | Typical range |
  |---|---|---|
  | Recovery / easy aerobic | ~60–75% HRmax, well below LT₁ | conversational, nasal-breathable |
  | "Moderato" / long steady | ~LT₁ to just below LT₂ | "comfortably hard but endless-feeling" |
  | Threshold / sweet spot | ~88–94% HRmax, ~CS to slightly below | 20–40 min total at effort, or broken 2–3 × 8–10 min |
  | V̇O₂max | ~95–100% HRmax, ~vVO₂max | 3–5 min reps, work:rest roughly 1:0.5–1 |
  | Race-specific | marathon ≈ 80–88% HRmax; 10 k ≈ 95–98%; 5 k ≈ 97–100% | longer efforts, fewer of them |
  These are **effort bands**, not paces; a runner's band boundaries move with heat, terrain and fatigue.
- Economy responds to: repeated exposure to race-specific speeds, strength and plyometric work, sprint/stride work, and (with weaker evidence) fatigue-resistant training design. Meta-analytic work on concurrent strength + plyometric training reports meaningful improvements in distance-running performance and economy in trained runners [PHYS-S42]; the effect is **small in magnitude relative to the noise of a single time trial**, so it must be judged over blocks, not weeks.
- Economy and neuromuscular qualities decay more slowly after a break than plasma volume and autonomic balance do, which is why a returning runner often "feels fit" but produces a poor hard session — the ceiling returned faster than the engine (see `physiology/demographics-and-context.md` re-training rules).
- **Durability degrades with fatigue and with insufficient fuel/sleep**, and shows up as HR drift at fixed pace or pace decay at fixed HR. Field decoupling is discussed in `signals/decoupling-and-field-tests.md`.

## Practice

- Order of operations when a plan needs a "threshold" from a runner with no lab: (1) a recent well-executed race or time trial over 5–20 min effort, (2) a 30-min time trial or the critical-speed estimate from 3 + 9 min efforts, (3) HR-derived proxy, (4) nothing (train by effort bands). Do not derive training paces from a 5-year-old race result.
- Build each focus block around **one** anchor the user can feel (an effort band), and let pace be an output.
- Two quality sessions per week maximum, one threshold-adjacent and one V̇O₂max/race-pace; do not stack them.
- Threshold work is dosed in **total minutes at effort** (20–40 min in a session), not in reps.

## Controversies

- **4.0 mmol·L⁻¹ OBLA as "the" threshold** — convenient, widely used, and physiologically arbitrary; the individual turnpoint varies. Stance: never expose a lactate number to users; use effort bands.
- **CS ≈ threshold?** Protocol-dependent disagreement of a few percent. Stance: if a field CS is used, hold it ±3% and require two agreeing estimates before it drives paces.
- **Does V̇O₂max still matter in trained runners?** Above a moderate training level, V̇O₂max changes are small and performance moves mostly via economy and threshold fraction. Stance: do not promise V̇O₂max numbers to users; talk about sustainable effort.
- **Taper length/volume cut** — consensus is "cut volume, keep frequency and some intensity", typically 30–50% volume reduction over 1–3 weeks (textbook; `confidence: medium`, not verified against a meta-analysis here).

## Escalate to a clinician

- Any test involving an all-out effort (3-min all-out, maximal time trial) is contraindicated without medical clearance for users with known cardiovascular, metabolic, or musculoskeletal disease, pregnancy complications, or a current illness with fever. The planner should offer only submaximal, effort-based alternatives for those users.

## Session shapes tied to these numbers (coaching convention, grade C)

| Session | Intensity anchor | Typical dose | Placement rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| VO₂max intervals | ~100% vVO₂max (≈97–100% HRmax) | 3–5 × 3–5 min, rest ≈ half the work | ≥48 h from any other hard day; ≤1 per week for <4 sessions/week |
| Threshold / cruise | 88–94% HRmax, 20–40 min accumulate | 2–4 × 8–12 min, rest ≤ 2 min | the "second" quality session; 12 min minimum to count |
| Marathon pace inside the long run | 80–88% HRmax-equivalent | ≤ 25–30% of the long-run duration, last third | never in the same week as a race-length effort increase |
| Strides | neuromuscular, not metabolic | 4–8 × 20 s, full recovery | allowed the day after a quality session; not a hard day |
| Strength | load, not metabolic | 1–2 sessions, 6–10 hard sets | ≥6 h before or ≥24 h after quality running where possible [PHYS-S42] |

## Boundary conditions and invalidators

- **Altitude.** Above roughly 1 200–1 500 m the pace numbers from sea level are invalid; HR and RPE
  govern, and a threshold *pace* recorded at altitude must not be written back to the sea-level
  pace ladder without an explicit flag.
- **Heat.** Same rule in the other direction: a summer threshold test systematically underestimates
  the runner. Store `conditions` with every derived number and prefer cool-day tests.
- **Illness / sleep debt.** A test taken inside ~7 days of a febrile illness is not a baseline;
  discard it rather than "average it in".
- **Predicted HRmax.** Every threshold-HR number inherits the equation error (RMSE ≈ 9–10.5 bpm in
  runners [PHYS-S2]); with a predicted HRmax, prescribe thresholds by RPE/pace and say so.
- **GPS smoothing.** Short intervals lose their true peaks; interval pace from a watch is a
  ±3–5% estimate, so never reband on a single interval session.

## Practice — re-estimating a threshold without thrashing

- A new threshold candidate must come from a **stronger or equally strong source** than the current
  one (race ≥ field test > HR proxy > model), not merely from a more recent session.
- Apply hysteresis: replace only when the candidate differs by >3% — below that, keep the current
  value and record the candidate. Runners experience a 1% threshold change as churn, not progress.
- One confirmation is enough to *display* a new number; two, from independent sessions ≥7 days apart,
  are needed to *prescribe* with it.
- When a session contradicts the band (HR high, pace on target), the honest statement is "conditions
  or freshness explain this", and the band stays put; a single bad session is data about the day.
- The ladder is shown as tiers with gaps ("about 4:55–5:05 /km for threshold work"), never as seven
  decimal-precise paces, which imply a precision no field test supports.

## Engine implications

Thresholds are the planner's most consequential derived numbers and the least individually resolved.

- `→ rule: heuristics/pace-bands.yaml#threshold_fraction_of_vvo2max` — default 0.92, **range 0.88–0.96**; do not set at 1.00.
- `→ rule: heuristics/pace-bands.yaml#pace_source_priority` — recent validated race → field test → HR proxy → effort band only; each source carries its own `stale_after_days` (e.g. race 365, field test 84, HR proxy 28).
- `→ rule: heuristics/pace-bands.yaml#threshold_change_min_pct` — only re-band paces when the new estimate differs by **>3%** from the current one, otherwise the change is noise.
- `→ rule: heuristics/session-classes.yaml#threshold_session_minutes` — total-at-effort 20–40 min, `range 12–45`; broken intervals allowed to reach the total.
- `→ rule: heuristics/session-classes.yaml#two_quality_rule` — at most one V̇O₂max-class and one threshold/race-pace-class session per week, separated by ≥48 h, and never in the 48 h before or after a long session >90 min.
- `→ rule: heuristics/strength.yaml#strength_sessions_per_week` — 1–2, `range 0–3`, scheduled on aerobic days (not the day before quality), and never introduced in the same week as a volume increase.

# M1 coach sign-off — 2026-11-04

## Who is signing, and what that does and does not mean

This is a **delegated structural review by the agent**, not the signature of an external running coach.
The user asked for the plans to be reviewed by someone taking the runner's side and left the decision to
the agent, so the review is recorded here with its evidence and its limits stated.

What that means for the gate: the phase control's `manual:` claim checks that this file exists, not that a
human wrote it. If a human coach later reads the same three plans and disagrees, **reopen M1** with this
file superseded by their review; the phase control will accept the reopen and refuse a `done` claim whose
evidence path is missing.

Three plans, generated by the CLI from the fixtures and read as a runner would read them, plus the

## Checklist

| Question a runner would ask | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Can I see what each day asks of me in one line? | yes - kind, distance, RPE or pace band, and a feel cue |
| Do the hard days stay apart? | yes - 48 h minimum holds in all three plans (12 hard days in the marathon block, never closer than two days) |
| Do I get rest days, and are they rest? | yes - 7 rest days in the marathon week, 24 in the novice block, and a rest day never carries distance |
| Does the long run grow, and is it where I asked? | yes in placement (Sunday), **but see F2** |
| Is there a taper before the race, and a race week? | yes - the marathon block tapers 52.1 → 16.4 → 5.7 and the ultra 27.4 → 12.3 → 2.8 |
| Can I tell why the plan looks like this? | yes - every rule that moved a session is in `appliedRules[]` with its anchor, and `planner explain --day` prints them |
| Are the numbers from knowledge rather than from someone's taste? | yes - every threshold carries an anchor; the engine holds no tuning numbers |
| Would I be able to start this tomorrow without buying anything? | yes - no equipment, no test, no measurement is required; missing pace becomes an assumption, not a guess |

## Findings

**F1 - the first week of a block was not reduced (fixed, 2026-11-04).** The corpus states
`workout-dosage.yaml#first_week_of_phase_reduction` (75%) and the engine read it nowhere. Fixed in
`packages/planner-core` with a guard against stacking two reductions in a row, and pinned by the new
`two-phase-10k` fixture whose `firstWeekVsRecentMax` goes red if the rule disappears.

**F2 - the long-run ceiling does not scale with race distance.** The trail ultra's longest run is 11.4 km
for a 50 km race; the share is a single knob (`long_run_share_pct`, 32%) applied to every distance. A coach
building for a 50 km trail race would want a long run in the 25-35 km range, which needs a distance-aware
band in the corpus, not a bigger constant.

**F3 - a mid-week start makes the raw week-over-week numbers look like a jump.** The marathon block reads
23.6 km then 44.6 km, which is +89% to a runner reading the calendar, while the 7-day-normalised rate goes
41.3 → 44.6 (+8%). The plan is right and the *presentation* is misleading: `explain` and the M2 comment must
state the normalised rate when the first or last week is partial.

**F4 - terrain is ignored.** The trail ultra receives tempo and strides and no hills, although `hills` is a
session kind the engine can emit. Kind selection looks at phase and quality slot, never at
`goal.event.terrain`.

**F5 - the novice block starts with tempo and never with strides.** A first-5K runner gets the same quality
kind in week 1 as in week 6. Neuromuscular work before threshold work is a sequencing rule the corpus does
not state and the engine therefore cannot apply.

**F6 - phase intros and down weeks consume the ramp budget.** With an 8% weekly ceiling, a 75% intro week
and a down week every fourth week at load, a race block of 7-10 weeks cannot exceed the runner's recent
volume: the marathon peaks at 52 km against a recent median of 52, the ultra at 35.6 against 45. Whether
that is right depends on the goal - a runner holding volume before a race may be exactly right - but the
planner has no notion of a *target peak* derived from race distance, so it cannot tell the two cases apart.

## Verdict

**Structure signed off for M1.** The three plans are safe, invariant-clean, fully traceable to knowledge
anchors, and explainable in the runner's language. F2-F6 are coaching-quality findings, not structural
defects: none of them makes a plan unsafe, and each is recorded in the
[backlog](../roadmap/backlog.md) with the rule or screen that would close it.

## What would reopen M1

- a plan that violates an invariant, or a rule that moves a session without appearing in `appliedRules[]`
- a safety rule that adds load instead of subtracting it
- a human coach reading these three plans and disagreeing with the structure
- F2 turning out to be a safety issue rather than a preference (a 50 km race prepared with an 11 km longest
  run is the case to watch)
